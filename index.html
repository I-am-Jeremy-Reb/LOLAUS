▸ Calculation Details▼
▸ How the centroid is calculated▼

The intensity-weighted centroid locates the beam's centre of mass:

\(\bar{x}=\dfrac{\sum x\,I(x,y)}{\sum I},\qquad\bar{y}=\dfrac{\sum y\,I(x,y)}{\sum I}\)

Only pixels above 10% of the peak contribute, preventing read-out noise from biasing the result. The camera noise floor (histogram peak in the low-level range) is subtracted beforehand. Saturated pixels are replaced by the 5×5 neighbourhood median.

▸ How the Strehl ratio is calculated▼

The Strehl ratio S is the ratio of the intensity between an abberated and idealized focal spot. S = I_measured/I_Diffraction-Limited. S = 1 is the maximum corrseponding to a perfectly focused diffraction limited spot.

\[S = \frac{I_{\text{measured}}}{I_{\text{DL}}}= \frac{P/A_{\text{measured}}}{P/A_{\text{DL}}}= \frac{P}{\pi r₈₆.₅^2} \Big/ \frac{P}{\pi r_{\text{DL}}^2}= \left(\frac{r_{\text{DL}}}{r₈₆.₅}\right)^2\]

rDL is the theoretical diffraction-limited radius, r₈₆.₅ is the radius of the calculated 86.5% EE spot. P is the power of the system and A is the area. So we see for a fixed power, the Strehl ratio can be given by the square of the of the diffraction limited spot over the measured spot radius. We note this treatment of the focal spot is most accurate for nearly-Gaussian beams with mild aberrations.

▸ How ellipticity is calculated▼

Ellipticity uses the second-moment (variance–covariance) tensor of the intensity:

\(\sigma_{xx}=\dfrac{\sum(x-\bar x)^2 I}{\sum I},\quad\sigma_{yy}=\dfrac{\sum(y-\bar y)^2 I}{\sum I},\quad\sigma_{xy}=\dfrac{\sum(x-\bar x)(y-\bar y)I}{\sum I}\)

The eigenvalues \(\lambda_{1,2}\) of \(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\sigma_{xx}&\sigma_{xy}\\\sigma_{xy}&\sigma_{yy}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) give the principal-axis variances. The 1/e² semi-axes are \(w_{1,2}=2\sqrt{\lambda_{1,2}}\) and \(\varepsilon = w_\text{min}/w_\text{max}\in[0,1]\). A perfect circular beam gives ε = 1. Values ≥ 0.87 are generally acceptable; below 0.70 indicates significant astigmatism. The orientation angle θ of the major axis (degrees from horizontal) is computed from the dominant eigenvector.

The yellow dashed ellipse overlaid on the image represents the 1/e² principal-axis ellipse derived from the second moments.

▸ How % energy in DL spot is calculated▼

The % energy in DL spot is the fraction of total beam energy that falls within the diffraction-limited radius rDL:

\(\text{EE}_\text{DL} = \dfrac{\displaystyle\sum_{(x,y):\,d(x,y)\leq r_\text{DL}} I(x,y)}{\displaystyle\sum_\text{all} I(x,y)} \times 100\%\)

where \(d(x,y) = \sqrt{(x-\bar x)^2+(y-\bar y)^2}\) is the distance from the intensity centroid. This requires both Microns/px and DL radius (Microns) to be entered. Unlike the Strehl ratio (an area proxy), this directly sums measured pixel counts inside the DL circle, giving a photometrically grounded measure of how much energy is concentrated in the diffraction-limited core.
